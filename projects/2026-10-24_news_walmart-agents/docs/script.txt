Amazon is kicking AI shopping agents out of its store.
Walmart just built them a private entrance, with a spending limit.
On October twenty-first, Walmart announced the Agent Gateway.
AI agents can search Walmart and Sam's Club and check out for you.
Walmart says it already works with ChatGPT, Gemini and Meta's Muse.
Next month, every bot gets its own profile inside your account.
Right now, an AI agent shops like a very fast intern.
It opens the website, reads the page, and clicks buttons built for humans.
That's slow, and it breaks every time someone moves a button.
The gateway skips the website.
Agents plug in over MCP, a standard connector for AI tools, and get product, price and stock straight from Walmart.
Your profile sets the rules.
Approve every purchase, the default, or let the bot spend up to a limit.
Fifty bucks of paper towels, sure. Two hundred bucks of TV, maybe not.
Now, Amazon.
In September it blocked Meta's Muse for never identifying itself or asking permission.
Here's the part nobody says out loud: an agent never sees an ad.
No sponsored products, no impulse add-to-cart, no Prime upsell.
For Amazon, every agent order is an ad nobody saw.
Walmart is betting the other way: lose the ad view, win the order.
The catch: the gateway is an invite-only beta, and Walmart picks who gets in.
Open door, with a bouncer.
Here's the test I'd run before trusting any of this.
Ask three agents to reorder the same paper towels.
Log where each one buys, what it pays, and whether it keeps your usual brand.
If it switches brands, your loyalty program just lost to a spreadsheet.
Amazon can lock the door, but your customers' bots will just shop where they're welcome.
So treat your product feed like a storefront. The next shopper might not have eyes.
