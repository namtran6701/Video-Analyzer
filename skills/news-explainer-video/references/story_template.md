# Story Template

One story, six sections, one takeaway. Budgets assume ~200 wpm (3.33 words per second), measured from the reference.

## Topic filter

Channel lens: how AI and platforms change the way businesses get customers, operate and make money.

A story qualifies only if the answer to this is a clear yes: **will this change how someone builds, markets, sells, spends or works?** Economics stories qualify only when they hit ad spend, financing costs or consumer behavior directly. When picking between candidates, prefer the one with the clearest consequence and the best evidence you can put on screen.

## Section budgets

| Section | 60s | 100s (default) | 150s | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–5s · 15 w | 0–6s · 20 w | 0–8s · 25 w | The contradiction or consequence itself. No setup, no logo, no "in today's video". |
| Sting | 5–6s | 6–8s | 8–10s | Channel sting, 2s max, no narration over it (or one short line). |
| What happened | 6–15s · 30 w | 8–20s · 40 w | 10–28s · 60 w | Who, what, when. Confirmed facts only, with the source on screen. |
| How it works | 15–32s · 55 w | 20–48s · 90 w | 28–70s · 140 w | The mechanism, through one concrete example and one diagram. |
| Stakes + catch | 32–48s · 50 w | 48–75s · 85 w | 70–110s · 130 w | Follow the incentive: who wins, who's squeezed, what the announcement leaves out. |
| Field note | – | 75–90s · 45 w | 110–135s · 80 w | What you tried or would test, and what broke. |
| Takeaway | 48–60s · 35 w | 90–100s · 30 w | 135–150s · 45 w | One thing to watch, test or reconsider, plus the fixed sign-off line. |

Word budgets add up to about 300 for 100s, leaving ~10% for pauses before punchlines. If a section runs long, take it out of How it works, never out of the hook or the takeaway. Cut the field note at 60s; it needs at least 12s to land.

## The one-sentence argument

Write it before the script: "[Thing] changes [behavior] because [mechanism], which means [consequence] for [who]." If you can't fill it, the story isn't ready.

## Writing rules

- Every sentence does one job: **fact**, **mechanism**, **consequence** or **joke**. Delete sentences that only announce the next section.
- Write a causal chain, not a list: event → what it removes or changes → who captures the value → the limit that could slow it.
- Short sentences, concrete verbs, numbers spoken the way people say them ("a hundred bucks a month").
- Put the stressed word at the end of the sentence. That's where the cut lands.
- Keep company claims attributed ("OpenAI says...") until something independent backs them.
- The takeaway must pay off the hook. Reread the first line after writing the last.

## Hook patterns

| Pattern | Shape |
|---|---|
| Promise vs reality | "This tool promises X. Setting it up takes Y." |
| Hidden consequence | "This looks like a [small thing]. For [group], it's a [big problem]." |
| Unexpected loser | "Cheaper X sounds great, unless you sell expensive X." |
| Everyday frustration | "You did [the right thing]. The platform still wants [money]." |
| Contradiction | "[Number] says up. [Behavior] says down." |

The hook's claim must be supported later in the episode.

## Joke patterns

Write two or three strong jokes per episode before adding memes. Memes then punctuate the lines that are already funny.

| Technique | How |
|---|---|
| Understatement | Describe a big problem in deliberately mild words. |
| Self-deprecation | Put yourself in the absurdity ("I automated it. I now maintain six automations."). |
| Concrete analogy | Turn an abstract mechanism into a familiar situation. |
| Literal reading | Take a product name or marketing line literally for one beat. |
| Rule of three | Two real items, a third that undercuts them. |

Jokes go right after a fact, never in place of one. Let a key fact land unjoked so viewers know what you believe.

## Field note

The segment that makes the channel the user's. One of:
- "I ran this on a real [workflow / campaign / client task], here's what happened."
- "Here's the test I'd run before trusting it," with the exact test.
- "Here's where it broke," with a screenshot of it breaking.

Never invent results. If the user hasn't tried it, use the "test I'd run" form.

## Titles and thumbnail

Draft the title before the script. It promises a specific explanation with tension: a conflict, a consequence or a question. The thumbnail carries one idea: a platform plus a consequence, or a surprising comparison. Three title options in the brief, user picks.
