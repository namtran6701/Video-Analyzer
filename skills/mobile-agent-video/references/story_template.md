# Story Template

Eight acts that tell one story: the agent reaches out, shows it can do the work, earns trust in a run of claims, reports back, waits for your sign-off, and lands the result. Budgets are in beats (120 BPM, 12 frames at 24fps, 0.5s).

## Act budgets

| Act | 30s (60 beats) | 45s (90) | 60s (120) | 90s (180) | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ping | 6 | 10 | 12 | 14 | Banner → phone, the day's brief, an offer |
| Title | 6 | 8 | 8 | 10 | Wordmark + pill, line pass, promise line |
| Show it working | 14 | 16 | 16 | 22 + 26 | One exchange → result card. 90s gets a second one with a different card |
| Claims run | 8 | 16 | 22 | 30 | 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 claims |
| Reports back | | | 20 | 26 | Float and dock, summary card, the user's decision |
| Control | 12 | 16 | 16 | 22 | Chip claim, draft card, edit, live edit, whip scroll |
| Payoff | 6 | 8 | 8 | 10 | Celebration banner + confetti |
| Close | 8 | 16 | 18 | 20 | CTA, line to logo, URL, silent hold |

Every column sums to the runtime in beats (seconds × 2). If you change one act, take the difference out of the close's silent hold or a phone act's hold. At 30s, skip the CTA: the line draws straight into the logo.

## Claims

A claim names a behavior of the agent, never a feature name. The product is the implied subject ("It ..."). 2 lines, about 4 words a line, plain words, no exclamation marks.

| Slot | Pattern | Example (tone only, write your own) |
|---|---|---|
| Title promise | Benefit noun, short qualifier. One line | "Your day, already in motion" |
| Claim 1 | What it does before you do | "It catches / the small stuff" |
| Claim 2 | Continues claim 1 with "and": what it holds back | "and saves the rest / for later" |
| Claim 3 | Stands alone: it keeps working while you're away | "Close the app / it keeps going" |
| Control claim | One line; one word becomes the chip that proves it | "Every reply waits for you" (chip replaces "reply") |
| CTA | Action, then an invitation. 2 lines | "Try it early / and tell us what's next" |

## Chat writing rules

- **The agent does the talking.** 1 to 3 short sentences per bubble, specific: names, numbers, deadlines, what it already did and what it needs from you. Briefs are numbered lists with a bold lead-in per item.
- **The user is brief.** 2 to 8 words, warm ("Yes please", "Love it, ship it"), except one decision message in Reports back (up to ~30 words).
- **Show the output.** Every exchange ends in something you can see: a card, a draft, a file tile. Never a paragraph about what it will do.
- **One fictional universe.** One person and their small business or busy week, with 3 or 4 running threads (a deadline, a hire, a deal, a renewal). The Ping's brief seeds every later act, and the payoff closes one of its threads. Realistic, never lorem ipsum, never real customer data unless the user provides it.
- **Reactions are punctuation.** One per exchange, and they go both ways.
- Never name competitors.
